How this works, and what it cannot do

What the pipeline does

  1. Ingest. Every three hours, fetch each outlet's public feed and store the new items: outlet, canonical URL, title, opening sentence, publication time. Nothing is scraped and no article body is ever fetched.
  2. Group. Items from the last four days are compared by meaning. Pairs that look close are put in front of a language model, which answers one question: do these two report the same event, or merely the same subject? Two or more outlets on one event become a story.
  3. Compare. A language model reads the headlines and opening sentences of one story and writes down what each outlet claims and where they differ, quoting each outlet's own words.
  4. Check. A validator refuses anything it cannot verify. Every quote must appear character for character in the item it is attributed to. An analysis that fails is not published.

What it cannot do

  • It has not read the articles. Everything here comes from a headline and an opening sentence. An outlet may well answer in paragraph four something this site records as an omission.
  • It does not decide who is right. Outlets differ because they filed at different times, had different sources, or chose different details. The site shows the difference and stops.
  • Absence is not evidence. A story here means two outlets were matched; a story missing means they were not. That is a limit of the matching, not a claim about the news.
  • The outlet numbers are not scores. See any outlet page.

What it costs

Feeds are free. Grouping runs on a small model on a free GitHub Actions runner. The comparison runs on a Claude subscription rather than metered API calls. The whole thing is designed to run for the price of the subscription someone already has.

Where the data is

Every item, story, judgment and analysis is a plain text file in a public repository, and every number on this site can be recomputed from them.
